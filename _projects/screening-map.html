Certifying Ollivier–Ricci curvature signs

Problem. To decide, for every edge of a graph, whether its Ollivier–Ricci curvature lies below a threshold τ, while solving as few optimal transport problems as possible.

Ollivier–Ricci curvature costs one optimal transport problem per edge. In Proposition I, Tebe Nigrelli and I show that the Balanced Forman value of an edge certifies an interval for its lazy Ollivier–Ricci curvature with no transport problem at all, and that the edge’s 2-hop neighbourhood sharpens it.

Balanced Forman curvature was introduced by Topping and others (2022).

Certify-then-solve takes the 2-hop interval, intersects it with the certificate of Tian, Lubberts and Weber, and solves a transport problem only for the edges still undecided. The map shows one stage at a time, as the paper’s figure does. Each stage draws the edges it decides in colour, red where κOR < τ and blue where κOR ≥ τ, and the edges decided earlier in grey; edges still undecided are not drawn.

What you are seeing

On the paper’s graph at τ = 0, the sign query, the BF interval alone decides 52.8% of the edges, the 2-hop interval brings that to 63.4% and the TLW certificate to 73.8%, so 781 of the 2,985 edges need a transport problem. Almost everything the BF interval decides has κOR ≥ 0, and almost everything the 2-hop interval and the TLW certificate add has κOR < 0.

Moving τ changes which edges sit close to the threshold, and so which ones are hard. The chart shows, for every τ from −1 to 1, how the edges split between the four stages; the vertical line marks the current τ, and dragging along the chart moves it.

As in the paper’s code, every interval is widened by 10−9 against floating-point rounding before it is compared with τ, so an interval whose lower end is exactly τ does not decide its edge. That is the spike in the chart at τ = 0: on the paper’s graph, 92 of the 781 transport problems at τ = 0 are edges whose lower end is exactly 0.

How it is computed

Every interval and curvature comes from the paper’s code, Curvature-Transfer-Code, and every exact value from orcut, the solver behind the exact values in the paper.

This random geometric graph has 800 uniform points in the unit square, joined when closer than r = 0.0564, so that the expected degree 800πr2 is 8; it is restricted to its largest component and drawn at its points. It is seed 0 of four at this degree, the graph of the paper’s figure, and “Draw another graph” steps through the other three.
