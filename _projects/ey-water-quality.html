Water quality prediction from location and date

My solo entry to the 2026 EY AI & Data Challenge, which predicted water quality at South African monitoring stations from location and date alone and reached the semifinal.

The challenge asked for three measures (total alkalinity, electrical conductance and dissolved reactive phosphorus) given only each sample’s coordinates and date. My entry placed in the top 10 of 953 teams.

Approach

Features. Built at each location and date from public sources: Landsat surface reflectance and spectral indices through the STAC APIs of the Microsoft Planetary Computer, terrain, land cover, surface water, geology, climate and soil properties, and auxiliary chemistry from South Africa’s Department of Water and Sanitation.

Validation. Spatial GroupKFold cross-validation. A nearest-neighbour encoder of nearby chemistry is refit inside each fold, so that no fold sees its neighbours’ targets, and adversarial validation checks for shift between training and test locations.

The figure shows the idea: the land is cut into blocks and each block in turn is held out, so no station is predicted from targets in its own block.

Model. Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost), extra trees and a per-station LSTM, combined by a ridge meta-learner fit on out-of-fold predictions and averaged over several seeds.
